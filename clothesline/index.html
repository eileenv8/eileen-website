How it works

Find your colors and your lines, then build a closet that lasts.

Most color analysts stop at the palette. Most stylists skip the analysis. We do both, then help you shop it: your own closet first, secondhand second, and a short list of natural-fiber makers last.

  1. Step 1

    Find your colors

    We hold fabric drapes to your face in daylight and watch what your skin does. You leave with your season and a palette card.

  2. Step 2

    Find your lines

    A body-line read: the shapes, fabrics, and proportions that sit right on your frame, written up as a style guide you keep.

  3. Step 3

    Build the closet

    A closet edit at home, then a shop-along through the Mission and Haight with a list matched to your palette and lines.

Closet to closet

What leaves your closet can be someone else's best find.

During a closet edit, you choose what to let go. Good pieces in natural fibers get tagged by season, body line, size, and fiber, so a Soft Autumn client shops a rack that's already edited for her. After 90 days, anything unsold comes back to you or goes to donation, whichever you picked.

Who we are

Eileen and Ashley, two friends in San Francisco.

We'd rather teach you what suits you than sell you more clothes. Clients should leave able to shop on their own.

Eileen has spent years bringing people together, from Jubilee Studio to community workspaces and a regenerative farm. Ashley had the idea that started the closet loop: clothes leaving one closet should find the next person they suit.
